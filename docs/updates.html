WitnessBench audit illustrates hypothesis revision and testing

The author’s WitnessBench audit adds a qualitative example of discovery evaluation: selected Kimi-K3 trace excerpts propose alternative puzzle rules, revise hypotheses, and plan a low-cost probe. The author argues that uncontaminated rules matter for measuring discovery and reports that the same game ultimately reached all 20 levels.

SageAI announces Sage-A1.5 scientific-workflow training

SageAI announces Sage-A1.5, described as a 35B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters. The account says its training draws on scientific-discovery workflows and tens of thousands of synthesized environments, and highlights parallel research agents and evidence-based corrections. The accompanying figures illustrate research orchestration and report benchmark scores; they do not establish recursive self-improvement.

RIDAR: Safeguarding Research Integrity in Autonomous Research Agents via Automated Detection and Runtime Intervention

RIDAR detects research-integrity violations in agent trajectories and supplies corrective feedback during execution. It combines a research-lifecycle taxonomy, post-hoc auditing, and runtime monitoring with a final-deliverable review barrier.

Qiuyang Mang introduces FrontierCS 2 research evaluation

Qiuyang Mang introduces FrontierCS 2, a benchmark that removes a paper’s contribution from its released code and asks agents to outperform the original method using the authors’ metrics, without access to the paper or web. Its accompanying chart reports Claude Opus 5.5 leading the 12 displayed agents with a pass-rate value of 58.

MLE-World predicts experimental feedback for research agents

The authors introduce MLE-World, which predicts ML experiment feedback so research agents can explore candidate programs before executing them. Their overview figure reports that confidence-based execution gating reduces real execution by 32–40% while maintaining or improving plotted mean-percentile performance.
